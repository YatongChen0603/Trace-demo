CRAFT: Compiling Proactive Sensing Experiences into Executable Skills for Mobile Embodied Agents

CRAFT lets a mobile agent decide what to sense next, then compiles that decision chain into a skill the device replays with no model inference between sensing calls. Above is one real night recording from the vehicle testbed: a person outside the car names a parking bay by speech and gesture, and the 2 billion-parameter on-device brain commits to slot_4 after six sensing calls and 3.6 seconds of its 12-second budget. Overlays are the site's bay map, the arm bearing recovered by the gesture tool, and the committed bay. Faces are blurred in every recording on this page.

Abstract

Mobile embodied agents operate in physical environments where task-relevant information is dispersed across heterogeneous sensors, may appear only briefly, and can sometimes be obtained only through physical interaction. This calls for proactive sensing: deciding at runtime what to sense or do next based on observations collected so far. However, invoking the model after every sensing step incurs substantial latency and energy overhead and may cause the agent to miss the narrow time window in which transient evidence is available.

We observe that different task instances often require similar sequences of sensing and action. Building on this observation, we present CRAFT, a proactive-sensing framework that uses cloud compute during training to build a skill compiler and an initial skill library, then deploys both on the mobile device. At runtime, all processing remains on the device: tasks matched to a reliable skill execute it locally without model inference between sensing steps, whereas unmatched tasks fall back to on-device model deliberation. A common tool interface unifies sensing and action, while outcome-based skill selection and confidence-aware fallback sustain the system reliability.

Across seven embodied datasets and two physical testbeds spanning vehicles, robots, and wearables, CRAFT reduces end-to-end latency by 42.7% while preserving task accuracy. These savings grow with the number of sensing steps, avoiding up to 51.1% of model invocations and 45.5% of generated tokens.

Why a Device Cannot Just Encode Everything

Evidence in a physical scene is sparse, brief, and spread across sensors that each cost something to read. In the parking exchange above, the spoken command and the pointing gesture each last a couple of seconds and never overlap. Consulting one stream at a time meets both windows. Encoding all three streams on every round takes so long that the speech is over before the encoder is free, and the gesture is over before the next round begins.

System Overview

Cloud training produces the skill compiler and an initial skill library. After deployment, the device retrieves, executes, and updates skills on its own. The system has four parts:

  1. One tool interface for sensing and action. Cameras, microphones, depth, IMU, gaze, trackers, and the arms themselves are typed JSON tools behind long-lived HTTP services. Adding a sensor changes a YAML contract rather than agent code, so the same brain moves between a car, an arm, and a headset.
  2. Deliberate once, in the cloud. The brain is trained by distillation from a large omni-modal teacher and then by reinforcement learning against outcome rewards, so a chain ends in a committed answer or action rather than in a description of what it saw.
  3. Compile the chain into a skill graph. The compiler keeps the calls, their order, and the guards that decide when a branch applies, and drops the prose. The executor and its guard evaluator are plain Python, under 300 lines, and need no GPU. Graphs live in a SQLite library alongside their retrieval embeddings.
  4. Serve by measured outcome. A skill is admitted, kept, or evicted on what it scores. At serving time a gate chooses between replay and deliberation, and a confidence-aware fallback catches the cases replay should not own. Removing that fallback costs 38 points of mean accuracy, which is what makes this a system rather than a cache.

What limits the real-car number. These rows run the lane where the gesture tool returns the labelled bay's bearing plus the noise the training environment adds, and a procedure-perfect chain there reaches 91.2%. In the harder lane, where the bearing is recovered from the arm in the forward camera, the reading carries a median error of 18.8 degrees, a procedure-perfect chain reaches only 36.9%, and CRAFT reaches 17.1%. That lane is measuring the gesture reader, not the policy.

Recordings from the Testbeds

Sentry, real car, garage at night. The car sits parked and unattended while a person walks up and bends over the bonnet. The brain decides which stream to wake and when what it has justifies a verdict, instead of encoding every camera continuously. Ground truth for this session is alert, labelled from the recording rather than by the model.
Why the choice matters, CARLA. Four cameras, twelve seconds, one spoken command: “This spot is no good, take that middle spot.” Only CAM_LEFT ever contains the person pointing. The bars are the windows in which speech and pointing are actually available, and a chain that samples outside them comes back empty.
Dual-arm manipulation, RealMan RM75-6FB. CRAFT chooses where to look, which arm senses and which acts, and what to measure: it reads a 0.436 m gap, asks for a 0.100 m push, and closes the loop on the 0.134 m the box actually moved. A visuomotor policy with the same cameras but no choice of viewpoint produces no box motion once the box leaves its wrist view.
An unseen cabin task, same 2B brain. Seven recorded cabin classes, 150 sessions, two questions the brain was never trained on. Answering what is making that sound needs the audio-event tool: without it the brain scores 0.427, below the 0.467 majority class, and with it 0.760.

Code and Data

The prototype is over 15,000 lines of Python built on PyTorch, verl, and vLLM. Tools run as long-lived HTTP services with typed JSON interfaces, so each specialist model is loaded once and shared across training, evaluation, and serving. The skill library is a SQLite file holding each graph as canonical JSON together with its retrieval embedding, which makes a library easy to copy and evaluate reproducibly.

Code, the skill libraries, and the recorded vehicle and cabin sessions will be released after review. This page is kept free of author and institution identity until then.
